# ADR 0012: Add a bounded AI flight instructor

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Supersedes:** None
- **Superseded by:** None

## Decision

V3 will include an optional authenticated AI flight instructor built on DeepSpace's `ai-chat` feature. The instructor will help the player understand the simulator through:

- a short preflight briefing based on the selected planet and initial conditions
- a post-flight debrief grounded in the verified flight record and control history
- answers to questions about the player's own saved flights and the documented simulation rules
- no more than two prioritized, actionable suggestions after a flight

The instructor will not control the craft, change simulation inputs, determine landing outcomes, or write and delete flight records. Immediate in-flight warnings such as low fuel, unsafe speed, excessive horizontal drift, or a poor landing angle will remain deterministic interface cues generated by the simulation.

The AI receives bounded, structured context: planet configuration, named landing thresholds, initial conditions, verified final telemetry, outcome reason codes, and selected control-history summaries. Numerical claims must come from that context or read-only tools. When the AI service is unavailable, the simulator and deterministic result explanation continue to work.

## Alternatives considered

- Exclude AI and rely only on deterministic explanations.
- Call an AI model continuously during live flight.
- Let the AI operate the controls as an autopilot.
- Send the complete simulation state on every frame.
- Add a general-purpose assistant with permission to modify all app records.

## Rationale

Preflight and post-flight coaching give the AI enough time and context to produce useful guidance without putting a variable-latency service in the control loop. Grounding the debrief in a server-verified flight lets the instructor explain what happened rather than guess. The feature demonstrates a DeepSpace integration that improves the learning experience while preserving the deterministic simulation as the source of truth.

Using the platform's existing authenticated chat and conversation-history support avoids rebuilding identity, streaming responses, and chat persistence. Restricting the instructor to read-only flight access matches its role and reduces the risk of unintended data changes.

## Tradeoffs

- The instructor cannot react conversationally to every control movement in real time.
- Generated coaching can still vary between runs even when the flight does not.
- AI usage adds latency, cost, prompt design, and failure states.
- Maintaining both deterministic warnings and AI coaching requires clear labels so players understand which source produced each message.

## Consequences

- The landing simulator remains fully usable without AI.
- Physics, collision, thresholds, and outcomes remain deterministic and never depend on model output.
- V3 will add the DeepSpace `ai-chat` feature rather than hand-building chat storage and streaming.
- The instructor's system prompt will define it as a concise coach for this specific simulator.
- Its tool allowlist will be read-only and limited to the current user, flight records, schemas, and documented rules.
- A flight identifier will connect a debrief to one verified V3 flight record.
- The interface will distinguish deterministic live warnings from AI-generated coaching.
- The application will expose AI usage deliberately, avoid automatic per-frame calls, and handle unavailable or failed responses without blocking flight.
- AI autopilot, automatic control changes, voice interaction, and background coaching during every simulation tick remain outside V3.
